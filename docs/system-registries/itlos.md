# ITLOS — International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea

Dossier on registry-like systems for governing-body mandates. Part of the
survey behind the `/system` mandate-registry column; taxonomy and scoring
scheme in [`../un-system-registries.md`](../un-system-registries.md).

## Systems

- **None scored.** ITLOS is a judicial body, not a mandate-issuing governing body. Its own output is case law (judgments, advisory opinions, orders), a different category from governing-body mandates/decisions; its governing body, the Meeting of States Parties to UNCLOS (SPLOS), takes only administrative, financial (budget) and electoral decisions, with no decision/mandate-tracking system.

## Related portals (not linked to mandates)

- **List of Cases** — <https://www.itlos.org/en/main/cases/list-of-cases/> — structured docket of contentious cases and advisory proceedings, with pleadings, orders, judgments and advisory opinions per case; a case-law database, not a mandate registry.
- **Documents of the Meeting of States Parties (SPLOS)** — <https://www.un.org/Depts/los/meeting_states_parties/meeting_states_parties.htm> (and ITLOS's own SPLOS archive) — annual reports, budget proposals, audit reports; administrative/financial governance documents, not decision-linked mandate tracking.

## Notes

- The UN system chart carries ITLOS with `on_display=FALSE` (it hides it) — flagged as an open question below.
- The judgments-vs-mandates distinction is the core point: a tribunal's "decisions" are adjudications of disputes and advisory opinions, i.e. case law binding on the parties to a case — not standing mandates to a secretariat that could be tracked as active/superseded/implemented. So a "mandate registry" is conceptually inapplicable here; the correct representation is a null registry with an explanatory note.
- Governing body: Meeting of States Parties to UNCLOS (SPLOS) — elects judges and adopts the budget; 172 States Parties. Grouping: `related-organization`.
